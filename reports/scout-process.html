AgentTrace / method explainer

From conversation traces to collaboration judgments

Scout supplies the model-based analysis machinery. AgentTrace defines the three categories, selects evidence, validates references, and displays the findings.

1. Analysis process

Green stages use an LLM. Structural metrics are a separate offline analysis, not inputs that mechanically determine the three judgments.

Sources: method documentation · scanner implementation · separate structural metric definitions.

2. Scout message types

AgentTrace's Scout annotation prompt assigns one primary type and up to two secondary types to each target message. These labels describe observable communicative function, not quality or success. Annotations also include inferred replies, addressees, confidence, ambiguity, rationale, and verbatim evidence spans.

Definition source: message-type definitions and annotation rules; output fields: MessageAnnotation schema.

Message typeDefinition used in the prompt
questionAsks for information, evaluation, clarification, or a decision.
delegation_requestAssigns or requests concrete work from another agent.

critique_disagreementChallenges, rejects, or identifies a problem.

clarificationResolves ambiguity or corrects interpretation.
artifact_sharingPublishes or points to a file, link, run, or other artifact.

social_heartbeatGreeting, availability ping, or low-content presence update.

How types feed the three metrics: primary and secondary types matching the scanner's relevant-type sets select a shared pool of candidate messages plus adjacent turns. The model then checks candidate text against all three failure definitions. A type is not itself a failure: for example, result_delivery does not establish that the result was integrated.

These are AgentTrace-defined, model-judged collaboration-failure categories, not built-in Scout metrics or deterministic scores. The operational definitions below come from the scanner prompt.

Interpretation matters. found=true means the model judged the supplied evidence sufficient. found=false can mean insufficient evidence, not demonstrated absence. Confidence is model-reported, not a calibrated probability.

Categories may overlap: nine traces produce 27 category judgments, not 27 distinct failures. Citation validation checks traceability, not semantic correctness. In the October 16 report, some saved rationales count purposeful verification as duplication despite the definition excluding it; human adjudication is needed.

4. Other analysis: distinct signals and labels

Offline structural analysis counts question marks and lexical agreement, challenge, and coordination markers, alongside lengths, timing, and turn structure. These are text-based signals, not semantic message-type classifications; they do not assign Scout's 15 types. See message-level metric definitions.

Human audit codebook: commsfail also provides the separate discourse_v1 codebook below. These are human-labeling options, not an additional automatic Scout stage or results established by the report. Each label is a separate yes/no decision per post, unlike Scout's primary/secondary classification.

Human discourse labelCodebook definitionDefinition source
accept The author makes an unconditional commitment to own a named item. Offers, intentions, and hedges do not count. Codebook definition
result The author asserts that a named item is delivered or ready for review. Label the assertion; whether the artifact exists is read from the record. Codebook definition
review_pass The author positively reviews another seat's current result for a named item. Intentions, questions, failures, and self-review do not count. Codebook definition

The separate regex_v1 annotator reports failure-mode evidence from text patterns; its failure-mode labels should not be confused with these message functions.
